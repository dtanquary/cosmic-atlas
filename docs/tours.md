# Guided tours

Two scripted routes in `src/data/tours.json` move the camera between real destinations and show a caption at each stop. The travel paths are camera motion only: the interpolated pose between two stops is not a physical trajectory, a flight time or a light path, and nothing about the catalog changes while a tour runs. Captions state the distance and the lookback time of each stop and say what is measured and what is assumed or illustrative. Every number a caption quotes is checked by `tests/tours.test.ts` against the data it comes from: the nearby layer, the pinned Milky Way frame, the DR1 manifest, the Planck18 CMB reference, the DESI name index and the cluster sidecar. A stop whose caption quotes another galaxy's distance names it in `cites` (the Local Group stop quotes Andromeda; the M32 and M110 stop quotes M110).

Each stop also has a stable route-scoped `id` and a short `cue` describing what to look for. Cues contain no new numerical claims; the source-checked explanations remain intact. The progress control opens **Stops & pace**, which pauses playback and offers a chapter selector, **Quick**, **Relaxed** and **Manual**, **Explore here**, and **Return to stop**. Quick retains the original dwell (eight seconds unless a stop overrides it); Relaxed doubles dwell without changing travel duration; Manual uses Prev/Next and disables automatic Play. Changing pace alone never resumes a paused tour. Chapter jumps and Return to stop use the normal cancellable visit and arrive paused. Explore here closes options and leaves the tour paused; Play resumes a non-manual tour, returning to its framing first if exploration moved the camera.

Pace is remembered only during the page session. Existing `#tour=road-trip` invitations deliberately retain the established automatic Quick start even after the recipient chose Manual during that session. No tour operation writes display preferences. Phone controls reuse the progress/cue nodes; the cue stays visible in the compact portrait panel and moves back with the title on desktop restoration. In short landscape viewports it is deferred until Details is expanded.

Lookback times follow the existing rule: Planck18 lookback for redshift-derived positions (DESI rows, the survey reach, the cluster center and the CMB shell) and distance ÷ c for the independently measured nearby layer and the Milky Way. Framing distances are the camera's distance from the stop target; `Explorer` computes the overview and CMB framings itself, the other stops carry theirs in the data.

### How the runner plays a route

`src/tour.ts` drives the explorer's animated visits and never moves the camera itself. Each stop travels for its own `travelSeconds` (the first one too, from wherever the camera is), then dwells for `dwellSeconds`, 8 s unless a stop sets its own, before advancing. Only an arrival reported as complete dwells: orbit input, flight or another focus taking over a travel pauses the tour at the stop, a drag or wheel during the dwell pauses it at once (the app listens to the controls' start event, and the runner's pose check at hop time is the backstop), and Pause, Prev, Next, Play, Exit and Escape do what they say; Exit and a mid-travel Pause stop the explorer's travel. The Sun, Local Group and cluster stops are camera-only framings (`applyView` from the recorded target; Sun and Local Group use the Milky Way approach direction, and Coma looks outward along the observer-to-cluster direction); the Milky Way, nearby and overview stops use the standard visits, and the M32 and M110 stop pulls the Andromeda visit back to 0.16 Mpc. Entering the CMB stop shows the shell through an app hook without saving the setting; leaving the stop or exiting the tour restores the saved choice rather than forcing the shell off. While a tour runs, its panel takes the right column and the galaxy and Milky Way inspectors and the CMB card wait until exit.

Catalog stops carry only a name. The runner resolves it through the loaded DESI name index (an app hook) to that index's verified `{id, node, row, targetId}`; `visitCatalog` then loads the checksum-verified metadata chunk and re-checks the exact target ID before moving. When the index does not match the active catalog (development subsets, bootstrap) or the name is missing, the stop is skipped with a notice naming it, in the direction of travel. Nearby stops work with every dataset because the nearby layer is independent of DESI.

The two survey overview captions contain the token `{catalogCount}`, which the runner replaces with the active manifest's accepted-row count (14,140,375 for full DR1, 1,000,000 for the development subset), so a subset never claims the full-release figure. The farthest-distance figure in those captions is written from the full DR1 manifest; the deterministic development subset rounds to the same 4,831 Mpc.

### Coma cluster row count

`scripts/prepare_tours.py` counts accepted DR1 rows within 2° of the NED center on the sky and within |Z − 0.023997| < 0.01, reading only the checksum-verified leaf metadata chunks whose bounds can hold such rows (9 of 882 leaves) through the shared `leaf_metadata` helper. The result, 2,110 rows, is recorded with the window in `tour-sources.json` under `desiMembers`; the script exits non-zero below 200 rows so the stop cannot silently frame an empty region. This is a sky-and-redshift window, not a membership catalog, a virial radius or a completeness statement, and it depends on where DESI observed. Abell 2199, the Hercules cluster and the Perseus cluster were checked the same way once by hand when the stop was chosen (Perseus, near the Galactic plane, has no accepted rows); Coma had by far the most.

## Regeneration

```sh
npm run data:tours
npm test
```

`prepare_tours.py` reads `src/data/tour-sources.json` and the full DR1 manifest and leaves under `public/data/dr1`. It converts the cited redshift to a Planck18 comoving distance, writes the cluster stop's `positionMpc` into `tours.json`, and writes the row count into the sidecar. Everything else in `tours.json`, including titles, captions, framing distances, timings and the Local Group midpoint, is hand-written and pinned by the tests to the data it quotes. Changing a caption number means changing the underlying source first (for example a nearby distance in `nearby-sources.json`, then `prepare_nearby_galaxies.py`) and letting the test tell you which caption now disagrees. Changing the cluster means updating its NED fields and reference codes in the sidecar and rerunning the script.

## Coma framing

The camera looks outward along the observer-to-Coma direction from 15 Mpc in front of the cited center. This makes the central gathering easier to identify than the previous 9 Mpc Milky Way approach angle, which looked obliquely back toward the observer through the redshift-distance elongation. The framing is illustrative; it neither compresses distances nor asserts membership. Individual observations retain their points and existing automatic models.
